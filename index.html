What the official feed gets wrong

The DMRC feed on otd.delhi.gov.in was last updated 10 Aug 2023 and expired on 31 Dec 2025. The map ignores the expiry and runs the weekday timetable every day.

Fixed when building the map

  • Six stations sat 0.9 to 4.4 km from where they really are (Adarsh Nagar, Mundka Industrial Area, Rajendra Place, Haiderpur Badli Mor, Sarojini Nagar and Samaypur Badli), and the feed's track was drawn through those wrong points. That made Yellow Line trains double back near Model Town and produced the impossible 196 km/h and 104 km/h runs. They now use Wikipedia's coordinates.
  • Two runs were still faster than a train can manage with an 80 km/h top speed: Noida Sector 148 to Knowledge Park (3 km at an average of 90 km/h) and Janpath to Central Secretariat (1.5 km in 83 s). They're lengthened to the fastest possible time, and the time comes out of the rest of the trip, so departures and arrivals at the ends don't change.
  • The 59 Red Line trips labelled "saturday" are run every day.
  • The feed's trains run 12 to 15% slower end to end than Google Maps shows today. Running times on the Red, Yellow, Blue, Violet and Airport lines and the Green Kirti Nagar branch are scaled so end-to-end rides match Google Maps (24 Nov 2026, 11:00): for example Samaypur Badli to Millennium City Centre takes 82 min, not 94, and New Delhi to Dwarka Sector 21 takes 21 min, not 27. Each train still leaves its first station at the published time, so trains are as frequent as before.

Signs the timetable is a model, not an operating plan

  • Every trip on a route has exactly the same running times all day: rush hour takes as long as 11 pm.
  • Every stop has a 20-second dwell, including interchanges like Rajiv Chowk.
  • There's no Sunday service. "Saturday" holds only 59 Red Line trips, which look like mislabelled weekday trips.
  • Red Line Rithala → Dilshad Garden has just 13 trips (the reverse has 159).
  • Off-peak and late-evening headways are identical on every line, and the feed puts more Yellow Line trains on the track at 11:30 pm (72) than at 9 am (39).

Apart from those six stations, the geometry is good: stations sit a median of 16 m from the drawn track.

Field protocol

  1. Stations (one per line, plus one terminal): Rajiv Chowk (Blue), Kashmere Gate (Red), Hauz Khas (Yellow), Botanical Garden (Magenta), Lajpat Nagar (Pink), Mandi House (Violet), Ashok Park Main (Green), Dhaula Kuan (Airport), and a terminal such as Dwarka Sec 21 where the timetable starts.
  2. Windows: 08:30–10:00 and 18:00–19:30 (peak), 13:00–14:00 (off-peak), 22:00–23:00 (late). That's 30 minutes per platform per window, both directions.
  3. What to log: the moment the doors open. Tap "Train arrived now" with this page's clock set to Live. Phone clocks are network-synced to within about a second, which is plenty.
  4. Sample size: about 30 arrivals per line and time window. That gives a stable median and a rough 90th percentile.
  5. Metrics: per line and window, the median and 90th percentile of |gap|, and observed versus scheduled headway. Gaps can't exceed half the headway, so headway error is the stronger test. At a terminal, also note whether the first train of the day leaves at the scheduled time. That's the only point where the timetable's phase can be checked directly.
  6. Pass bar: the map is "good enough" for a line when its median gap is under a quarter of the headway and observed headway is within 20% of scheduled.
